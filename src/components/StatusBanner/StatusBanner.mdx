Fills are local to the banner. They are close to `Tag`, but not the same, so the two do not share a palette.

## Location on the page

- If the message affects the entire page, place it above the relevant navigation. That is the sticky banner.
- If it only affects a specific section, table, form, or workflow step, place it within that section. That is the in-line banner.
- Steppers: below the title and subtext.
- Pages with tabs: below the tabs, above the table.

## How to use

### In-line

Render `StatusBanner` in the section it describes. It scrolls with that content. Pass `items` when the message is several specific issues. That shape is in-line only.

```tsx
<StatusBanner
  type="error"
  title="2 Missing Costs"
  description="Bid lines missing costs are highlighted below."
  items={[{ title: "Framing" }, { title: "Drywall" }]}
/>
```

### Sticky

Use a sticky banner when the message applies to the whole page and should stay pinned under the header. The layout sets `sticky`. Callers pass the banner content and leave `sticky` unset. Item rows stay on the in-line banner.

How a page layout accepts that content is TBD. It lands with the layout updates.

## Anatomy

A simple banner is a tag, a title, descriptive text, and actions. The actions are a primary and a secondary button.

A detailed banner adds a chevron, and each row can carry a text link in its subtext and an outlined call to action.

Closed, the detailed banner uses the in-line chrome plus the chevron. Open, it becomes a white card with a 2px type border, a 16px radius, and a tinted header strip. Rows sit under the header, separated by a hairline. The panel height animates, and the chevron rotates with it. While closed, the rows stay out of the tab order and the accessibility tree.
